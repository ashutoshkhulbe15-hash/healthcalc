# Coffee and Caffeine During Pregnancy

[ACOG advises keeping total caffeine intake below 200 mg per day during pregnancy](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy). Count caffeine across drinks and foods; this is a total daily amount, not an amount for each drink.

## Coffee amounts vary

A cup is not a fixed measure of caffeine. Serving size, coffee type, preparation and strength change the amount. Check the cafe or product information when available and include it in your daily total. ACOG describes 200 mg as roughly the amount in one 12-ounce cup of coffee; that comparison is approximate and does not describe every cup. See [ACOG’s patient explanation](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/having-a-baby).

Tea, chocolate, cola, energy products and some medicines can also contain caffeine. See the [drinks guide](/guides/drinks-pregnancy) for the same total-intake guidance. This page does not classify a specific brand or make a claim that a particular intake guarantees an outcome.

## What the limit means

ACOG's statement is that moderate caffeine consumption below 200 mg daily does not appear to be a major contributing factor in miscarriage or preterm birth; the relationship with fetal growth restriction was undetermined in that Committee Opinion. It is not a guarantee of zero risk, nor a reason to interpret symptoms or pregnancy outcomes from caffeine intake alone.

ACOG notes caffeine can be present in some medicines, including a product containing acetaminophen and caffeine; include verified amounts in the daily total. See [ACOG’s medicine example](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/headaches-and-pregnancy). Ask a healthcare professional about an individual concern.

*Educational information based on ACOG guidance. This page does not estimate the caffeine in an unlabelled drink.*
