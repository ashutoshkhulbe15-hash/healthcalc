# Pregnancy Weight Gain: Guideline Ranges and Their Limits

This page explains US guideline ranges using pre-pregnancy BMI. It does not infer fetal weight, growth or an individual treatment plan from a maternal scale reading.

## Total-gain guidance for one baby

The [National Academies’ US guideline, Summary Table S-1](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/12584/chapter/2), gives these singleton total ranges based on pre-pregnancy BMI:

| Pre-pregnancy BMI | Total gain, pounds |
|---|---|
| Below 18.5 | 28–40 |
| 18.5 to below 25 | 25–35 |
| 25 to below 30 | 15–25 |
| 30 and above | 11–20 |

These are US population reference ranges, not a required reading at a particular week. [CDC’s summary](https://www.cdc.gov/maternal-infant-health/pregnancy-weight/index.html) displays its obesity row as BMI 30.0–39.9; the original report uses BMI ≥30. Do not infer that one range establishes an appropriate individual goal at every higher BMI. Twin guidance is separate; do not multiply a singleton range by two. The [calculator](/pregnancy/weight-gain-calculator) shows the supported guidance and its source notes.

## What the underlying guideline does

The [2009 National Academies report](https://www.nationalacademies.org/publications/12584) considers pregnancy outcomes and provides population guidance. It is not an individualized diagnosis or a guarantee that a particular gain gives a healthy outcome. A single scale reading cannot assess fetal growth or distinguish fluid from other tissue changes.

## Reading your own measurements

Keep the pre-pregnancy starting value and the dates of later measurements. A gain of 20 lb means current minus starting weight, not the baby's weight. Enter all measurements in the same unit system. The calculator clears measurements when units change to prevent a kg value being interpreted as pounds.

A person beginning at 132 lb and measuring 148 lb has gained 16 lb. That arithmetic alone does not establish whether the gain is appropriate at their stage of pregnancy or for their medical circumstances.

## Symptoms deserve attention

Discuss persistent weight loss, difficulty eating or drinking, or a concerning trend with your care team. Severe headache, vision changes or extreme swelling of hands or face are among the [CDC urgent maternal warning signs](https://www.cdc.gov/hearher/maternal-warning-signs/index.html); do not explain them away with a weight-gain chart.

*Educational guidance. Your care plan should account for your pregnancy and health history.*
