# Drinks During Pregnancy: Caffeine and Food-Safety Sources

Different drinks raise different questions. This guide limits its advice to the linked US guidance on caffeine, alcohol and unpasteurized juice. It does not assign a pregnancy-safe dose to herbal teas, matcha, energy drinks or kombucha because we did not verify a major guideline supporting those page-specific limits.

## Caffeine

[ACOG advises less than 200 mg caffeine per day during pregnancy](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy). Count total caffeine from foods, drinks and medicines. Amounts vary by serving and preparation, so use product or seller information when available. See the [coffee page](/pregnancy/safe-food/coffee) and [matcha page](/pregnancy/safe-food/matcha); neither gives a product-specific dose without reliable label information.

## Alcohol

The [CDC says there is no known safe amount or safe time for alcohol during pregnancy](https://www.cdc.gov/alcohol-pregnancy/about/index.html). Labels and products differ; this page does not certify a particular beverage.

## Juice and unpasteurized products

CDC's [safer food choices for pregnant people](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) lists unpasteurized juice or cider as a riskier choice and pasteurized juice as a safer choice. Follow the label and handling instructions. This page does not assess homemade juice or an unlabelled product.

This guide does not assign safety ratings or serving limits to herbal teas, supplements, energy drinks or kombucha.

*Educational information based on US guidance. Product labels, local advice and individual circumstances matter.*
