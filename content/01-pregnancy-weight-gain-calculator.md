# Pregnancy Weight Gain Calculator

## What this calculator shows
Enter height, pre-pregnancy weight and current weight in one unit system. BMI uses converted kilograms and meters. Gain so far is current minus pre-pregnancy weight. Switching units clears measurements so they are not reinterpreted.

The calculator displays U.S. National Academies total-gain ranges, also presented by CDC, for one baby or twins. The National Academies says these guidelines are intended for use in the United States and should be used with clinical judgment; its twin ranges are provisional. The underweight twin range is an exception: CDC cites a separate study, while the National Academies says evidence was insufficient to develop a twin range for underweight pregnancies. Higher multiples need an individual plan. These totals are not targets for your current week; no weekly curve is inferred. [National Academies: guideline table, U.S. scope and provisional twin ranges](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/12584/chapter/2); [CDC: ranges and underweight twin exception](https://www.cdc.gov/maternal-infant-health/pregnancy-weight/index.html).

The pound ranges match the linked reference. The kilogram range displayed beside them is an approximate arithmetic conversion from pounds, rather than the separately rounded kilogram range in the National Academies table.

## How to use the result
Discuss your trend and personal circumstances with your maternity team. Weight change alone cannot assess pregnancy health. Seek clinical advice for concerning symptoms rather than relying on this calculator. [National Academies: use the ranges with clinical judgment](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/12584/chapter/2).
