# Stress and the Body: What NIH Sources Say

Stress can cause physical and emotional reactions. The U.S. National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) says the body's stress response includes release of hormones and changes such as faster heart rate, breathing and blood pressure, muscle tension and sweating. It describes occasional stress as a normal coping response. [NCCIH: Stress](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/stress).

NCCIH says long-term stress may contribute to or worsen digestive disorders, headaches and sleep disorders, and may worsen asthma; it also reports links with depression, anxiety and other mental illnesses. This describes possible associations and worsening, not a claim that stress inevitably causes organ damage in an individual.

## Approaches described by NCCIH
NCCIH summarizes evidence for relaxation techniques, breathing, mindfulness and yoga, while noting that evidence varies by approach and outcome and that safety limits apply. Its page says people who are struggling to cope or whose symptoms do not go away may want to speak with a professional. See the linked source for evidence quality and cautions. This article does not prescribe a stress treatment.
