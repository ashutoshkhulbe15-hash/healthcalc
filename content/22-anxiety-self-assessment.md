# GAD-7 Anxiety Screening Questionnaire

The GAD-7 is a seven-item self-report instrument asking about symptoms over the previous two weeks. The NIH lists it as an initial screening tool, not a diagnostic result. The exact questionnaire and response options are shown above; the total is a symptom-screening score, not a diagnosis or an estimate of an individual's probability of having a disorder. [NIH instrument record](https://www.nih.gov/node/19876).

## Score bands and study context
The original validation study described scores of 5–9, 10–14 and 15–21 as mild, moderate and severe symptom-score bands. Those labels describe the instrument's score grouping; they do not establish a clinical diagnosis or treatment need. In that study of adult patients at 15 US primary-care clinics, a threshold of 10 had 89% sensitivity and 82% specificity for probable generalized anxiety disorder compared with a mental-health professional interview. The study reports that performance in its particular sample; it is not a universal accuracy estimate for every country, age group, or care setting. The study's positive predictive value at that threshold was 29% in its sample, illustrating why a screen cannot be read as an individual diagnosis. [Original validation study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/410326).

## Use and limits
This website reproduces the GAD-7 questionnaire and calculates its simple item sum. It does not diagnose a condition, evaluate other causes of symptoms, or recommend treatment. If symptoms concern you, discuss them with a qualified health professional; the score alone cannot decide whether evaluation is needed.
