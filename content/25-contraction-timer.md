# Contraction Timer

This timer records how long a contraction lasts and the interval from the start of one contraction to the start of the next. These are time measurements only: the tool cannot determine whether labor has begun or when you should travel for care.

## Using the timer

Tap **Start Contraction** when a contraction begins and **Contraction Ended** when it ends. Start the next one when it begins. The history shows recorded durations and start-to-start intervals; averages are arithmetic summaries of the entries recorded in this browser session.

Follow the instructions from your maternity team, since advice may depend on your pregnancy and local care arrangements. ACOG advises contacting your care team if you think labor has started or you are unsure, and contacting them if your water breaks. The timer is not a reason to delay that call. [ACOG: How to Tell When Labor Begins](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/how-to-tell-when-labor-begins).

ACOG advises going to the hospital if your water has broken without contractions, you have heavy vaginal bleeding, constant severe pain with no relief between contractions, or reduced fetal movement. [ACOG: when to call or go to hospital](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/how-to-tell-when-labor-begins).

If you may be before 37 weeks and have possible labor symptoms, contact your maternity team promptly. [ACOG: Preterm Labor and Birth](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/preterm-labor-and-birth).

## Limits

Timing alone cannot confirm cervical change, labor stage, or whether a contraction is Braxton Hicks or labor. This timer does not make a hospital-arrival decision. Do not use an average or timer result to decide to wait for care.

This tool is informational and does not replace medical evaluation. If you are uncertain or concerned, contact your maternity team.
