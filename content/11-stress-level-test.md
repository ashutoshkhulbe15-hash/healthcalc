# Perceived Stress Scale information

The Perceived Stress Scale (PSS) is a self-report questionnaire about perceived stress. Carnegie Mellon University's scale page states that it is not a diagnostic instrument and has no score cutoffs; its scores are intended for comparisons within a sample. The author-maintained page also directs users to request permission to use the questionnaire ([CMU scale information](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/stress-immunity-disease-lab/scales/index.html)).

The PSS-10 scoring guidance says to reverse the four positively stated items and then sum all ten item scores ([CMU scoring instructions](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/stress-immunity-disease-lab/scales/html/pssscoring.html)). Because questionnaire use permission has not been confirmed for this site, this page does not reproduce the items or provide a scoring form. Use the official source for the instrument and its permission process.

The scale is not a diagnosis. If stress or distress is affecting your life, consider discussing it with a qualified health professional. A questionnaire score should not be used to delay support.
