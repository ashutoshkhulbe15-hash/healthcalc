# Meat and Deli Foods During Pregnancy: Match the Rule to the Food

Raw meat, whole cuts, ground meat and ready-to-eat deli products are different categories. One visual test or fixed microwave time cannot establish that all of them have been prepared appropriately.

## Ready-to-eat deli products

[CDC Listeria guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html) recommends reheating deli meats, cold cuts, hot dogs and fermented or dry sausages to 165°F or steaming hot for people at increased risk, including pregnancy. It also lists premade deli salads as a category to avoid, with homemade versions as the alternative.

The whole portion needs the stated preparation. Toasted bread, browned edges or a particular brand do not establish that the meat inside has been heated appropriately.

## Raw meat and cooking temperatures

[FDA pregnancy cooking guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/cooking-food-safety-moms-be) distinguishes whole cuts, ground meat and poultry. Use the relevant temperature instruction and a thermometer. The [USDA chart](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart) lists 145°F plus a three-minute rest for beef steaks/chops/roasts, 160°F for ground meat and 165°F for poultry.

The [steak page](/pregnancy/safe-food/steak) explains that distinction. “Crispy,” “well done” and “warm” are not interchangeable with a measured temperature instruction.

## Why no fixed microwave duration is given

The needed heating time depends on the product, portion and appliance. A statement such as “30 seconds removes the risk” cannot be transferred reliably to another sandwich or microwave. Follow the package directions and check the required result rather than treating a timed example as a guarantee.

## An accidental exposure

A previous meal does not let this page predict illness or pregnancy outcome. Do not make decisions from an invented probability. For a recalled product, follow the recall instructions; if symptoms occur or you are concerned, contact a healthcare service and explain the food and timing.

*Educational US food-safety guidance. Preparation, storage, contamination and applicable local advice all matter.*
