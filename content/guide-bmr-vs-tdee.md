# BMR, REE and TDEE: How the Terms Differ

Basal metabolic rate (BMR) refers to energy use measured under strict basal conditions. Resting energy expenditure (REE) is measured under less restrictive resting conditions; the National Academies notes that the terms are sometimes used interchangeably, although their measurement conditions differ. Total energy expenditure (TEE, also called daily energy expenditure) includes resting energy, the thermic effect of food and physical activity. [National Academies: energy expenditure definitions](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK591031/).

## What an equation estimates
Predictive equations estimate resting energy from characteristics such as age, sex, height and weight. Mifflin–St Jeor was developed for resting energy expenditure in adults; its output is not a direct measurement of BMR or total daily expenditure. [Mifflin et al. (1990)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/).

Some calculators multiply a resting-energy estimate by an activity factor to produce a total-energy estimate. The selected factor is an assumption in the model, not a personal measurement. Neither a BMR/REE estimate nor a TDEE estimate establishes a suitable calorie intake for a particular person.
