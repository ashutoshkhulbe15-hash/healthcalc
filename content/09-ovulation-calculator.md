# Ovulation Calculator: An Estimated Six-Day Fertile Window

The calculator uses a calendar model rather than detecting ovulation. Enter the first day of your period and a whole-number average cycle length from 21–45 days. Count the first period day as cycle day 1.

## The model and a worked example

Estimated ovulation is **cycle day = cycle length − 14**. This is an assumption, not a measurement of your luteal phase. The estimated six-day window includes the five days before estimated ovulation plus that day.

For a 28-day cycle beginning October 1, the model gives cycle day 14, or October 14. The displayed window is October 9–14, six dates inclusive. The next period is estimated at October 29. Those are arithmetic outputs for the example, not a guarantee that ovulation occurs on October 14.

## What the evidence supports

[ASRM's 2022 committee opinion on natural fertility](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/optimizing-natural-fertility-a-committee-opinion-2021/) describes a six-day fertile interval ending on ovulation. It also explains that timing varies, including with apparently regular cycles, so calendar predictions cannot establish the actual window in a particular cycle. Ovulation tests and cervical-mucus observations provide additional information but are not a guarantee of conception.

## Important limits

Do not use this calculator to identify safe days for contraception. It does not confirm ovulation, identify infertility or give an individual pregnancy probability. Irregular cycles and other circumstances can make its assumptions unsuitable.

The 21–45-day input range is a software limit, not a definition of normal cycles or a range in which predictions are clinically validated.

*An educational date estimate. Use an appropriate contraception method if pregnancy is not desired, and seek individual advice for fertility concerns.*
