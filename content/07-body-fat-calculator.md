# Military Circumference Equation Estimate

This calculator applies the circumference equations documented in the U.S. Army's 2013 regulation for body-composition calculations. Those equations were developed for military body-composition assessment; this page presents their output as an estimate and does not diagnose a condition or define a healthy body-fat range.

The source equation uses inches and base-10 logarithms. For men, it uses abdomen circumference minus neck circumference and height. For women, it uses waist plus hip minus neck circumference and height. The tool converts centimetres to inches before applying the equation. [U.S. Army Regulation 600-9, Appendix B, Table B-5](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/566071.pdf).

The regulation's female worked example appears arithmetically inconsistent: evaluating its stated equation with its listed sample measurements gives about 47.3%, while the printed worked result says 46.73%. This calculator follows the equation coefficients and full-precision calculation; it does not copy that worked-example total.

## Interpreting the result

This equation produces a model estimate from tape measurements. Its source population and purpose do not establish clinical accuracy for every person or a health category for an individual. The National Academies describes the military services' body-composition assessment methods and the service-specific context in which circumference equations were developed ([National Academies, Body Composition and Physical Performance](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/6104/chapter/4)).

The calculator does not show “essential,” “athletic,” “fit,” or “obese” classifications because this page does not have a directly applicable authoritative source establishing those categories for this method and for individual health interpretation. It also does not give a personal target.

Enter measurements according to the field labels. Small measurement errors can change a mathematical estimate; repeatable measurements do not turn the equation into a clinical measurement. If you need body-composition information for a health decision, discuss an appropriate assessment with a qualified professional.

## Formula source

U.S. Department of the Army. *Army Regulation 600-9: The Army Body Composition Program*, June 28, 2013, Appendix B, Table B-5, sample calculations. [Official PDF](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/566071.pdf).
