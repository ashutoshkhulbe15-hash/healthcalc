# Soft Cheese During Pregnancy: Type and Preparation Matter

“Soft cheese” is a broad category. Do not assume that a pasteurization label alone settles every cheese question. Identify the type of cheese, how it will be served and the guidance you are following.

## Current US advice

[CDC's Listeria prevention guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html) lists pasteurized cottage cheese, cream cheese, string cheese, feta and mozzarella as safer choices. It advises against unheated queso-fresco-type cheeses even when pasteurized, and against raw-milk cheeses served unheated. The relevant heating instruction is 165°F or steaming hot.

## Mould-ripened and blue cheeses

[UK NHS pregnancy guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/) distinguishes ordinary pasteurized soft cheeses from mould-ripened white-rind cheeses such as brie and camembert, and soft blue-veined cheeses. The latter groups should be thoroughly cooked until steaming hot, even when pasteurized.

National recommendations do not use exactly the same categories. This page identifies the source rather than presenting one blanket rule as a universal FDA/ACOG statement.

## A practical label check

Write down the actual cheese name, whether the milk is pasteurized and whether the dish will be served cold or thoroughly heated. A package saying “pasteurized” answers one question; it does not say which preparation category the cheese belongs to. If you cannot identify the product, use a clearly described alternative.

For example, a cold portion of pasteurized mozzarella and an unheated queso-fresco-type cheese should not receive identical advice just because both labels say pasteurized. Read the product category in the linked guidance.

## What this page does not establish

It does not confirm the safety of a specific brand, restaurant dish or recalled product. A category summary is not certification of an individual product.
