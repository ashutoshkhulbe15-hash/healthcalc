# Lean Body Mass Equation Estimate

## What the number represents
This page calculates the Boer height-and-weight equation estimate in kilograms. It is an equation output, not a measured body-composition result. A peer-reviewed study of 215 adults treated for head-and-neck cancer compared this equation with CT-based body-composition assessment and found that the height-and-weight equations did not adequately approximate CT results in that population. That study does not establish performance for every population; it does show why this estimate should not be treated as an individual measurement.

## Equation
For the male equation: 0.407 × weight (kg) + 0.267 × height (cm) − 19.2. For the female equation: 0.252 × weight (kg) + 0.473 × height (cm) − 48.3. The calculator converts imperial inputs to kilograms and centimeters before applying the formula. Its displayed pound value is a unit conversion of the equation result.

As an arithmetic example, 80 kg and 180 cm with the male equation produce 61.42 kg (about 135.4 lb). This worked example demonstrates the formula only; it is not a reference value or health target.

## Limits
The source study evaluated adults with head-and-neck cancer receiving radiotherapy; its results should not be generalized to populations it did not study. The equation estimate is not a diagnosis, body-fat percentage, nutrition prescription, or substitute for a measured assessment. This page does not calculate fat mass or compare multiple formulas.

## Sources

[Original Boer paper (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6496691/) describes estimating lean body mass from body weight and height to normalize body-fluid volumes; its abstract does not reproduce the equation.
