# Everyday Food Handling During Pregnancy

This guide covers a few food-handling recommendations that US public-health agencies specifically address. It does not label foods absent from these sources as universally safe or unsafe.

## Produce and sprouts

CDC recommends washing fresh fruits and vegetables and lists raw or undercooked sprouts as a riskier choice; cooked sprouts until steaming hot are the safer choice. Follow the [CDC pregnancy food-safety table](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html) and [FDA produce-washing advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/cleaning-food-safety-moms-be). Washing does not replace the specific cooking instruction for sprouts.

## Juice and dairy

CDC lists unpasteurized juice or cider and raw milk products as riskier choices, with pasteurized juice and dairy as safer choices. Check the label; do not infer pasteurization from the product name. See CDC's [safer food choices](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html).

## Eggs and prepared foods

CDC lists raw or undercooked eggs, including foods containing them, as a riskier choice. FDA advises cooking eggs until yolks and whites are firm and using pasteurized eggs or egg products in recipes served raw or undercooked. See our [egg guide](/pregnancy/safe-food/eggs) and [FDA egg-safety advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/what-you-need-know-about-egg-safety).

This guide does not assess other foods, supplements, herbs, allergens or prepared dishes. Follow applicable product labels, recalls and local public-health advice.

*Educational information based on US CDC and FDA guidance; not an assessment of a particular food or exposure.*
