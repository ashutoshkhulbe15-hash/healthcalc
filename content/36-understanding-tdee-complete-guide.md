# Understanding Total Daily Energy Expenditure

Total energy expenditure (TEE) is the energy used over a day. The National Academies describes three main components: resting energy expenditure, the thermic effect of food, and physical-activity energy expenditure. Physical activity includes both exercise and non-exercise movement; its contribution varies. Fixed percentages should not be assumed for an individual. [National Academies, Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy, chapter 4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK591031/).

## Resting-energy equations and total expenditure
Resting energy expenditure (REE) is not the same measurement as total expenditure. Mifflin–St Jeor is a predictive equation developed to estimate resting energy expenditure from age, sex, height and weight in adults. It does not directly measure an individual's daily total. [Mifflin et al. (1990), original study record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/).

A calculator that applies an activity factor to an REE equation produces a model-based estimate. Its result depends on the equation and selected activity factor; it is not a measured energy requirement, a verified maintenance intake, or a personal calorie prescription. This page does not provide a weight-loss target or a method for adjusting food intake.
