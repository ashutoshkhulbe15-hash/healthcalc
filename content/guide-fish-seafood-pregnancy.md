# Fish and Seafood During Pregnancy: FDA/EPA Categories

The US [FDA/EPA fish advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/questions-answers-fdaepa-advice-about-eating-fish-those-who-might-become-or-are-pregnant-or) recommends that adults who are or may become pregnant eat 2–3 servings per week from the “Best Choices” category, choosing a variety. One adult serving is four ounces. The FDA says fish provide nutrients and recommends choosing by category rather than avoiding all fish.

The category depends on the fish type. For example, the chart lists salmon, cod and canned light tuna among Best Choices; albacore and yellowfin tuna among Good Choices; and bigeye tuna among Choices to Avoid. Other species and some fish with the same common name can differ. Use the current chart for the specific type instead of relying on a summary list here.

For self-caught fish, check local, state or tribal advisories. FDA/EPA state that when no advisory is available, adults should limit the fish to one serving in that week and eat no other fish that week. Follow the current page for full instructions.

## Cooking and raw fish

FDA says finfish should be cooked to 145°F (63°C), or until opaque and flaky when a thermometer is not available or appropriate. FDA also provides specific visual guidance for shellfish. ACOG advises avoiding raw or undercooked fish during pregnancy. “Sushi-grade” wording does not replace those instructions.

Canned and ready-to-eat products still require label and storage directions. The mercury chart does not certify preparation, contamination controls or an individual product.
