# Protein reference for healthy older adults: ESPEN guidance

The European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism (ESPEN) practical guideline says protein intake for older persons should be at least 1 g/kg body weight per day and should be adjusted for nutritional status, physical activity, disease status, and tolerance. Its commentary reports that 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day has been suggested for healthy older persons. These statements are in the guideline's general principles, recommendation 2 and commentary (2022, pp. 959–960). [Read ESPEN's practical guideline (PDF)](https://2022.espen.org/files/ESPEN-Guidelines/ESPEN_practical_guideline_Clinical_nutrition_and_hydration_in_geriatrics.pdf).

## What this calculator does

Enter body weight to see the arithmetic equivalent of 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day. For example, at 70 kg, 70 × 1.0 to 70 × 1.2 gives 70–84 g/day. The calculator converts pounds to kilograms using 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, then rounds the range endpoints to whole grams.

This is a European population reference for healthy older persons, not an individualized target. The calculator does not cover illness, malnutrition, frailty, recovery, kidney disease, or other clinical circumstances. ESPEN says older persons' protein amounts should be individually adjusted for relevant factors and notes that needs in frail and ill older people are difficult to assess. Obtain individualized nutrition guidance for those circumstances. [ESPEN practical guideline, recommendation 2 and commentary](https://2022.espen.org/files/ESPEN-Guidelines/ESPEN_practical_guideline_Clinical_nutrition_and_hydration_in_geriatrics.pdf).

## Regional guidance differs

The United States' current 2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines state a protein serving goal of 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day, adjusted as needed for individual calorie requirements. That is a different national guideline and should not be combined with ESPEN's healthy older-person range. [U.S. Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025–2030, p. 2](https://cdn.realfood.gov/DGA.pdf). This page presents only the ESPEN reference above.

This tool does not assess nutritional status, diagnose a deficiency, or recommend a treatment or supplement dose.
