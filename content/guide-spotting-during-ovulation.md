# Spotting Around Ovulation: Timing Does Not Establish the Cause

Light bleeding between periods can occur around mid-cycle, but timing and colour alone cannot identify why you are bleeding. This page does not diagnose “ovulation spotting” or “implantation bleeding” from a calendar date.

## What to record

If you notice bleeding, record when it began, its amount and duration, associated pain, medications or contraception, and whether pregnancy is possible. These observations help communicate the issue; they are not a self-diagnosis checklist.

## When to seek advice

The [NHS advises getting bleeding between periods or after sex checked](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/vaginal-bleeding-between-periods-or-after-sex/). Possible causes include hormonal contraception, polyps, fibroids and sexually transmitted infections. Assessment depends on your circumstances.

A missed period together with unusual bleeding and abdominal or pelvic pain needs urgent assessment because ectopic pregnancy is one possible cause. Sudden severe pain with dizziness or fainting requires emergency care. A positive pregnancy test is not required before seeking help for concerning symptoms. [NHS ectopic-pregnancy symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ectopic-pregnancy/symptoms/).

## Related information

The [ovulation calculator](/pregnancy/ovulation-calculator) gives an estimated calendar window; it cannot identify the cause of spotting. If testing for pregnancy, follow your test's instructions rather than relying on the appearance of bleeding.
