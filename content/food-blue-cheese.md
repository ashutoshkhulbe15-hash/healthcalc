# Blue Cheese During Pregnancy: Check the Type and Heating Guidance

The name “blue cheese” does not establish the milk treatment or serving preparation. A pasteurized label is useful information, but this page does not use it as an automatic green light for every blue-veined cheese.

## The guidance used here

[NHS pregnancy advice](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/) says soft blue-veined cheeses such as Danish blue, gorgonzola and roquefort should be thoroughly cooked until steaming hot, whether the milk is pasteurized or not. That is a cheese-category instruction, not simply a milk-label test.

[CDC Listeria prevention advice](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html) separately identifies raw-milk cheeses as a concern and gives 165°F or steaming hot as the heating instruction for the listed cheese categories. The guidance should not be paraphrased as a guarantee that every pasteurized cheese is safe cold.

## Questions to ask about a dish

Identify the cheese used and whether it is a cold dressing, a garnish added after cooking or a dish heated thoroughly. Cheese that melts on contact with a warm item is not evidence that the whole portion reached the stated condition. Ask about the preparation rather than relying on the menu name alone.

If the cheese category or preparation cannot be confirmed, select a clearly described alternative. For other cheese types, see the [soft-cheese guide](/pregnancy/safe-food/soft-cheese).

## Limits

This page does not certify a particular brand or restaurant meal. Its NHS and CDC recommendations are identified separately because national guidance can differ.
