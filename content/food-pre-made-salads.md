# Pre-Made Salads During Pregnancy: Identify the Product Category

A bag of leafy greens and a premade deli chicken salad are different products. Advice about one should not be generalized to every item sold as “salad.”

## Premade deli salads

[CDC Listeria prevention guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/prevention/index.html) lists premade deli salads, including coleslaw and potato, tuna or chicken salad, among foods to avoid for people at increased risk. It gives homemade deli salads as the alternative. “Freshly made” or an expensive brand is not a separate safety category in that advice.

## Leafy salads and preparation

Use the preparation and storage instructions applicable to the actual product. [FDA’s pregnancy cleaning guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/cleaning-food-safety-moms-be) addresses fruits and vegetables. [FDA’s chilling guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/tips-chill-food-food-safety-moms-be) addresses refrigeration and storage. A homemade item still depends on its ingredients, handling and refrigeration; homemade does not mean risk-free.

## Read the whole dish

Check what is included: a salad can also contain cold deli meat, soft cheese, raw fish or an egg-based dressing. The relevant guidance depends on those ingredients and their preparation, not simply on the leaves being present.

When choosing a product, check the label and any recall or storage instructions. This page cannot confirm the handling of a particular container, supermarket counter or restaurant kitchen.

## Limits of a safety label

A green category in a food checker is a summary, not certification of a product. If you have eaten a recalled item, follow the recall guidance and seek advice for symptoms or concerns rather than using a generic “salad is safe” statement as reassurance.
