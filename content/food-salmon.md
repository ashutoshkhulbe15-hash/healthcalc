# Can I Eat Salmon During Pregnancy? Safety, Mercury, and Benefits

Salmon is one of the best foods you can eat during pregnancy. The FDA specifically recommends it as a "best choice" fish — low in mercury, high in omega-3 DHA, and rich in protein, vitamin D, and selenium.

The only rule: cook it to 145°F (63°C) internal temperature. No raw salmon, no sashimi, no poke bowls with raw salmon. Cooked salmon — baked, grilled, pan-seared, or poached — is considered safe by both FDA and ACOG guidelines for 2–3 servings per week.

## How Much Salmon Can You Eat Per Week While Pregnant?

The FDA recommends 2–3 servings (8–12 ounces total) of low-mercury fish per week during pregnancy. Salmon qualifies as a low-mercury fish with mercury levels of approximately 0.022 ppm — among the lowest of any commonly eaten fish.

This means you can eat salmon 2–3 times per week for the entire pregnancy without approaching any mercury concern.

> **Key Takeaway:** According to FDA guidelines, pregnant individuals can safely eat 2–3 servings of cooked salmon per week. Salmon has some of the lowest mercury levels of any fish (0.022 ppm vs. the FDA action level of 1.0 ppm) and is one of the richest food sources of DHA — the omega-3 fat critical for fetal brain development.

Many pregnant people avoid fish entirely out of mercury fear. This is counterproductive — the nutrients in salmon (particularly DHA) are so beneficial for fetal brain development that avoiding fish may actually be worse than eating it. The FDA's advisory explicitly encourages fish consumption during pregnancy, with the caveat to choose low-mercury species like salmon, shrimp, tilapia, and cod.

## Why Salmon Is Actively Recommended During Pregnancy

Salmon isn't just "allowed" during pregnancy — it's actively recommended by multiple health authorities. Here's why it matters for fetal development.

**DHA (docosahexaenoic acid)** is the standout nutrient. A single 3-ounce serving of salmon contains approximately 1,000–1,500 mg of DHA — far exceeding the 200–300 mg daily minimum recommended during pregnancy. DHA accumulates in the fetal brain and retina, with the heaviest accumulation happening in the third trimester.

Research by Oken et al. (2005) in the *American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology* found that higher maternal fish intake correlated with improved infant cognitive scores and neurodevelopment outcomes.

> **Note:** DHA cannot be synthesized by the human body in sufficient quantities — it must come from diet. Salmon is the richest common food source. A prenatal DHA supplement typically provides 200–300 mg; a single salmon serving provides 3–5x that amount. Whole food DHA also comes packaged with complementary nutrients (protein, selenium, B12) that supplements don't provide.

**Complete protein** — 17–20g per 3-ounce serving, all nine essential amino acids. Pregnancy increases protein needs by about 25g per day above baseline. Two salmon servings per week contribute meaningfully to that target.

**Vitamin D** — 450–570 IU per serving, covering 75–95% of the daily 600 IU requirement. Vitamin D supports fetal bone development, immune programming, and reduces risk of gestational diabetes and preeclampsia.

**Selenium** — 36–54 mcg per serving (60–90% of daily need). Supports maternal thyroid function and fetal thyroid development.

**Vitamin B12** — 2.4–3.2 mcg per serving, critical for fetal nervous system development and DNA synthesis.

## Salmon Cooking Temperature and Safe Preparation

All salmon must reach 145°F (63°C) internal temperature during pregnancy — verified with a meat thermometer at the thickest part, away from bone.

| **Cooking Method** | **Time for 1-inch fillet** | **How to check** |
|---|---|---|
| Baking (400°F oven) | 12–15 minutes | Thermometer reads 145°F; flesh is opaque |
| Pan-searing | 4–5 minutes per side | Thermometer reads 145°F; flakes easily |
| Grilling | 4–6 minutes per side | Thermometer reads 145°F |
| Poaching | 10–12 minutes | Flesh is fully opaque, no translucent center |
| Air-frying (400°F) | 8–10 minutes | Thermometer reads 145°F |

> **Warning:** Raw salmon — sashimi, poke bowls, ceviche, lox, and cold-smoked salmon — is not recommended during pregnancy. Raw salmon can harbor Listeria, Anisakis parasites, and Salmonella. "Sushi-grade" labeling does not eliminate these risks. Only thorough cooking to 145°F does.

**What about smoked salmon?** Hot-smoked salmon that reaches 145°F+ during the smoking process is considered safe. Cold-smoked salmon (lox) typically does not reach safe internal temperatures and should be avoided, or heated to 165°F before eating.

**Wild-caught vs. farmed:** Both are considered safe during pregnancy. Wild-caught salmon tends to have slightly higher omega-3 content; farmed salmon is more affordable and widely available. The nutritional difference is minor — either is far better than no salmon at all.

**Can I eat Atlantic salmon while pregnant?** Yes. Atlantic salmon (the most commonly farmed variety) has low mercury levels and high DHA content. It is explicitly included in the FDA's "best choices" category for pregnant individuals.

## Trimester-Specific Guidance

**First trimester (weeks 1–13):** Can you eat salmon during the first trimester? Yes — the same guidelines apply throughout. If fish aversions from morning sickness make salmon unappealing, mild preparations like poached salmon in a light broth may be more tolerable than heavily seasoned options. If salmon is truly intolerable right now, other protein and DHA sources can bridge the gap until the aversion passes.

**Second trimester (weeks 14–26):** DHA accumulation in fetal brain tissue accelerates significantly. This is an important window for consistent salmon intake. The FDA's recommendation of 2–3 servings weekly is most impactful when maintained through the second and third trimesters.

**Third trimester (weeks 27–40):** Fetal brain development continues intensively. DHA needs are highest in these final months. Continuing regular salmon consumption through delivery supports cognitive development at its most critical phase. The protein and vitamin D also support the increased metabolic demands of late pregnancy.

> **Bottom Line:** Salmon is one of the most beneficial pregnancy foods available — high DHA, low mercury, excellent protein. Cook to 145°F, eat 2–3 servings per week per FDA guidelines, and skip the raw preparations. Both wild and farmed salmon are good choices.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is salmon safe to eat during pregnancy?**

According to FDA and ACOG guidelines, cooked salmon is considered safe and actively recommended during pregnancy. It is classified as a "best choice" low-mercury fish. Cook to 145°F internal temperature and consume up to 2–3 servings per week.

**How much salmon per week is safe while pregnant?**

The FDA recommends 8–12 ounces (2–3 servings) of low-mercury fish per week during pregnancy. Salmon falls in this category. You can eat salmon 2–3 times weekly throughout your entire pregnancy without mercury concern.

**Can pregnant women eat cooked salmon?**

Yes. Cooked salmon — baked, grilled, pan-seared, poached, or air-fried to 145°F internal temperature — is considered safe throughout pregnancy according to FDA guidelines. Only raw salmon preparations (sashimi, poke, lox, ceviche) should be avoided.

**Is raw salmon safe during pregnancy?**

No. Raw salmon can contain Listeria, Anisakis parasites, and Salmonella. The FDA advises against all raw fish during pregnancy regardless of "sushi-grade" designation or restaurant quality.

**Can I eat salmon while pregnant in the first trimester?**

Yes. The same cooking and serving guidelines (145°F, 2–3 servings/week) apply across all three trimesters. If morning sickness makes salmon unappealing in the first trimester, it's fine to return to it when the aversion passes.

**Is farmed salmon OK during pregnancy?**

Yes. Both farmed and wild-caught salmon have low mercury levels and high DHA content. The FDA includes both in its "best choices" list for pregnant individuals.
