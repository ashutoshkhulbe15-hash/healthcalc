# Salmon During Pregnancy: Fish Categories and Cooking

The current [FDA/EPA fish advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/questions-answers-fdaepa-advice-about-eating-fish-those-who-might-become-or-are-pregnant-or) lists salmon among “Best Choices.” The US advice for adults who are or may become pregnant is 2–3 servings per week (four ounces per serving) of fish from that category in total. That is a combined weekly amount across Best Choices fish, not a separate allowance for each species.

## Cooking and raw fish

FDA says finfish should be cooked to 145°F (63°C), or until opaque and flaky when a thermometer is not available or appropriate. See the [FDA cooking guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/cooking-food-safety-moms-be).

ACOG advises avoiding raw or undercooked fish during pregnancy because it is more likely to contain parasites or bacteria than fully cooked fish. A label such as “sushi-grade” does not replace cooking guidance. See the [sushi page](/pregnancy/safe-food/sushi).

The FDA fish chart concerns mercury categories; it does not certify a brand, source water, storage history or restaurant preparation. Check local advisories for self-caught fish.
