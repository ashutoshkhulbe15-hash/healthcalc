# Alcohol During Pregnancy: Current CDC Guidance

The [CDC says there is no known safe amount or safe time for alcohol use during pregnancy](https://www.cdc.gov/alcohol-pregnancy/about/index.html). This includes beer, wine and spirits. The CDC also advises against alcohol when trying to become pregnant.

## Why the guidance applies throughout pregnancy

Alcohol can affect development throughout pregnancy. The CDC describes associations with pregnancy loss, preterm birth, stillbirth and lifelong fetal alcohol spectrum disorders (FASDs). Effects vary; it is not possible to predict from a particular exposure whether a baby will be affected. The guidance does not claim that every exposure causes harm.

## If you drank before knowing

The CDC says to stop when you learn you are pregnant, continue regular prenatal checkups and discuss concerns with your healthcare professional. Prior drinking does not establish that harm occurred. If stopping is difficult, the CDC recommends getting help; its page links to support resources.

## Drinks labelled non-alcoholic

Products and labels can differ. Check the actual product label and ask the manufacturer if its alcohol content is unclear. Do not assume a product is alcohol-free from its name alone. The CDC guidance above is about alcohol exposure during pregnancy; it does not evaluate a particular brand.

*Educational information based on US public-health guidance. This page cannot assess an individual exposure or pregnancy.*
