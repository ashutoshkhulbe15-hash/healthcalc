# Protein Guidance Depends on Population and Purpose

## Current U.S. dietary guidance
The U.S. *Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025–2030* give a protein serving goal of 1.2–1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight per day, adjusted as needed for individual calorie requirements. This is current U.S. federal population guidance, not a personalized prescription and not a supplement or treatment dose. Other countries and clinical situations may use different guidance. [U.S. Dietary Guidelines, 2025–2030, page 2](https://cdn.realfood.gov/DGA.pdf).

The National Academies' 2005 U.S./Canada Dietary Reference Intakes set a separate adult protein RDA of 0.8 g/kg/day. An RDA is a reference-intake value from that DRI framework; do not treat it as an individualized target or interchange it with the newer U.S. consumer guidance. [National Academies, DRI report, chapter 10](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10490/chapter/12).

## Resistance-training research is a separate question
A 2018 systematic review and meta-analysis included 49 studies with 1,863 healthy adults in resistance-training programs lasting at least six weeks. It reported that protein supplementation augmented training-related changes in strength and fat-free mass in those study settings. Its meta-regression found no additional increase in fat-free mass beyond total intakes of about 1.6 g/kg/day. This result concerns healthy adults doing resistance training; it does not set a universal protein requirement, establish a fat-loss dose, or show that supplements are necessary. [Morton et al. (2018), full abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/).

## Limits
This page does not calculate a personal protein target or address disease, pregnancy, lactation, adolescence, older-adult clinical care, or kidney conditions. The linked [protein intake reference calculator](/fitness/protein-intake-calculator) performs only the arithmetic for the current U.S. range; it cannot determine an individual's need. For older adults, see the separately sourced [ESPEN reference page](/body-metrics/protein-needs-seniors), which identifies its European population and clinical limits.
