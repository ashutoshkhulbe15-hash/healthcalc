# Eggs During Pregnancy: Cooking, Regional Advice and Choline

Egg guidance depends on preparation and country. U.S. CDC advice is to cook eggs until yolks and whites are firm, with pasteurized eggs for foods that will not be cooked to a safe temperature. The U.K. NHS makes a specific exception for qualifying hen eggs; that exception is not a worldwide rule for ordinary eggs. [CDC table](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html); [NHS egg section](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/)

Eggs can contribute choline, but their nutrient content does not override food-safety handling. NIH lists a large hard-boiled egg as supplying 147 mg of choline, and the U.S. pregnancy Adequate Intake is 450 mg/day. An intake reference is not an instruction to eat a fixed number of eggs. [NIH Tables 1–2](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Choline-HealthProfessional/)

## Are Eggs Safe During Pregnancy?

> **Key Takeaway:** Follow the actual preparation guidance. In the U.S., CDC distinguishes firm-cooked eggs from pasteurized eggs used in preparations that will not receive safe cooking. “Pasteurized” does not certify every ingredient or handling step in a dish. [CDC egg rows](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html)

| Preparation | U.S. CDC advice and context |
|---|---|
| Boiled, scrambled, fried or omelet | Cook until both yolks and whites are firm |
| Uncooked mousse or salad dressing | Use pasteurized eggs |
| Egg dish without meat or poultry | Cook to an internal temperature of 160°F |
| Egg dish containing meat or poultry | Cook to 165°F |
| Raw dough or batter | Egg choice does not remove the separate raw-flour issue |

[CDC egg and flour tables](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html)

**U.K. distinction:** NHS allows raw or partially cooked hen eggs bearing the British Lion stamp or produced under the Laid in Britain scheme, before the use-by date. Other hen eggs must be well-cooked, as must duck, goose and quail eggs. Check the actual stamp or packaging; do not infer eligibility from shell color, a farm name or the word “fresh.” [NHS current egg guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/)

CDC also recommends clean, separate, cook and chill practices. One cooking endpoint is not a guarantee that a meal handled incorrectly afterward cannot cause illness. [CDC prevention advice](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html)

## Why Eggs Are Excellent for Pregnancy

Eggs are one food source of choline. NIH explains choline's roles in cell membranes, nervous-system functions and early brain development. That does not establish that eating eggs will guarantee a developmental outcome, or that eggs are essential for everyone. [NIH choline introduction](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Choline-HealthProfessional/)

| Verified reference | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 147 mg choline in one large hard-boiled egg | NIH food-table value for that specific preparation and serving |
| 450 mg/day during pregnancy | U.S. Adequate Intake for pregnant adolescents and adults |
| Two such eggs: 294 mg | Our multiplication of the table value, not a prescribed daily portion |
| 294 ÷ 450 ≈ 65% | Our comparison with the pregnancy AI, not the food-label Daily Value |

[NIH reference and food tables](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Choline-HealthProfessional/)

NIH uses an **Adequate Intake**, rather than an RDA, for choline because evidence was insufficient to establish an RDA. It also lists choline sources other than eggs. If you do not eat eggs, that fact alone does not diagnose a deficiency or require a particular supplement. Discuss your overall intake and prenatal products with your care team. [NIH recommended intakes and food sources](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Choline-HealthProfessional/)

Check actual portions when comparing foods. A reference for one large hard-boiled egg should not be copied as the exact amount in a restaurant omelet, a smaller egg or a recipe containing several ingredients. Supplement labels also need their own check; a prenatal name does not establish its choline amount.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I eat eggs while pregnant?**

Follow your region's preparation advice. CDC recommends firm-cooked yolks and whites in the U.S., with pasteurized eggs for uncooked preparations. NHS has the specific qualifying U.K. hen-egg exception described above. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html); [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/)

**Are runny eggs safe during pregnancy?**

Ordinary runny eggs are a riskier choice in CDC's U.S. table. NHS's U.K. exception applies to hen eggs bearing the specified scheme identification, not all eggs and not duck, goose or quail eggs. Do not combine those recommendations into a universal permission. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/pregnant-women.html); [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/)

**How many eggs can I eat per day while pregnant?**

The verified sources here do not prescribe two or three eggs daily for every pregnant person. The choline example shows nutrient arithmetic, not a personal meal plan. Consider your overall intake, preferences, allergies and professional advice rather than deriving a quota from this food table. [NIH reference context](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Choline-HealthProfessional/)
