# Historical weight-equation comparison

The tool displays outputs from four historical equations that estimate a reference weight from height and sex: Devine, Robinson, Miller and Hamwi. The term “ideal body weight” appears in the original literature, but the outputs should not be treated as an individual's ideal, healthy, or target weight.

## Equations used

For height in inches at least 60 inches, the calculator implements:

| Equation | Male coefficient | Female coefficient | Units and source |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Devine (1974) | 50 kg + 2.3 kg per inch over 60 in | 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg per inch over 60 in | Metric output; historical drug-dosing paper |
| Robinson et al. (1983) | 52 kg + 1.9 kg per inch over 60 in | 49 kg + 1.7 kg per inch over 60 in | Metric output; derived from height-weight tables |
| Miller et al. (1983) | 56.2 kg + 1.41 kg per inch over 60 in | 53.1 kg + 1.36 kg per inch over 60 in | Metric output |
| Hamwi (1964) | 106 lb + 6 lb per inch over 60 in | 100 lb + 5 lb per inch over 60 in | Original U.S. customary equations, converted for display |

These coefficients are reproduced in a 2016 *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* paper comparing the equations and deriving a separate BMI-aligned alternative. That analysis explains that traditional equations can produce different weight estimates, are not aligned to one shared BMI, and do not define a universal ideal for all people. [Peterson et al., “Universal equation for estimating ideal body weight and body weight at any BMI”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4841935/)

## Interpreting the output

Each number is only the arithmetic result of the selected historical equation. The equation names and sex-specific coefficients do not establish a healthy weight, predict health outcomes, or recommend a weight-loss goal. The cited analysis describes traditional ideal-body-weight equations as having important limitations and notes that BMI ranges are more commonly used in clinical settings. [Peterson et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4841935/)

For context only, the calculator also shows the weight corresponding to adult BMI 18.5 to less than 25 at the entered height. CDC's adult BMI categories are screening categories for people 20 and older; they are not a personalized target. [CDC adult BMI categories](https://www.cdc.gov/bmi/adult-calculator/bmi-categories.html)

The calculator does not provide an adjusted body weight or medication dose. It does not account for an individual's clinical context.
