# Anxiety and Stress: What the Terms Mean

Stress and anxiety can share symptoms, but they describe different experiences. The National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) describes stress as a physical or mental response to an external cause. Anxiety can occur as a reaction to stress even when there is no current threat. Persistent apprehension or dread that interferes with daily life may warrant discussion with a health professional; this comparison cannot diagnose an anxiety disorder.

| Stress | Anxiety |
|---|---|
| Generally responds to an external cause, such as an exam or conflict. | Can continue without an immediate threat. |
| May resolve when the situation is resolved. | May involve persistent apprehension or dread that affects daily life. |
| Can affect sleep and cause tension or worry. | Can share symptoms such as worry, tension, headaches and sleep disruption. |

These descriptions overlap. A person cannot determine a diagnosis from this table or from an online quiz. NIMH advises considering professional help when symptoms persist or interfere with everyday life.
