# Blood Sugar Tests: Diagnostic Ranges and Limits

Age alone does not define a universal glucose target. Diagnostic thresholds concern specified laboratory tests; glucose-management goals for people with diabetes are individualized. The values below are diagnostic criteria for nonpregnant people, not home-meter treatment goals.

## Nonpregnant diagnostic tests

| Test | Prediabetes range | Diabetes threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Fasting plasma glucose | 100–125 mg/dL | 126 mg/dL or more |
| Two-hour plasma glucose after a 75 g OGTT | 140–199 mg/dL | 200 mg/dL or more |
| A1C | 5.7–6.4% | 6.5% or more |

These are laboratory criteria, not a diagnosis from one home-meter reading. In the absence of clear hyperglycemia, ADA guidance requires confirmatory testing. An ordinary meal is not a standardized oral glucose tolerance test.

## Pregnancy protocols differ

The ADA describes a one-step protocol using a 75 g OGTT at 24–28 weeks of gestation, after an overnight fast. In that protocol, gestational diabetes criteria are met if **any one** of these plasma glucose thresholds is reached: fasting 92 mg/dL, one hour 180 mg/dL, or two hours 153 mg/dL. ADA also describes a two-step 50 g screening test followed, when indicated, by a 100 g OGTT, with different diagnostic criteria. Local practice and professional guidance vary; use the protocol your maternity team orders and their interpretation.
