# Blood Sugar Levels by Age: Diagnostic Ranges and Treatment Targets

A blood-sugar number needs context: which test was used, whether you were fasting, whether you are pregnant, and whether you already have diabetes. Age affects screening and treatment decisions, but an older person's diabetes treatment target should not be labelled a normal result for someone without diabetes.

[NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis) separates diagnostic blood tests from home monitoring and explicitly states that an over-the-counter glucose meter cannot diagnose diabetes. This guide explains laboratory ranges and age-related questions without inventing different healthy fasting thresholds for every decade.

## Blood Sugar: Diagnostic Ranges and Age Context

> **Key Takeaway:** For nonpregnant people, the [NIDDK diagnostic table](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis) lists fasting plasma glucose **100–125 mg/dL** as the prediabetes range and **126 mg/dL or above** as the diabetes threshold. A diagnosis generally needs confirmation. These are laboratory criteria, not personalized medication targets or a chart for ordinary meals.

| Diagnostic category, outside pregnancy | Laboratory A1C | Fasting plasma glucose | Two-hour oral glucose tolerance test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Below the prediabetes threshold | Below 5.7% | Below 100 mg/dL | Below 140 mg/dL |
| Prediabetes range | 5.7–6.4% | 100–125 mg/dL | 140–199 mg/dL |
| Diabetes threshold | 6.5% or above | 126 mg/dL or above | 200 mg/dL or above |

[Source: NIDDK, nonpregnant diagnostic table](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis). The “below” row is an upper diagnostic boundary, **not a claim that any lower glucose value is healthy**; low blood sugar requires separate attention. The OGTT column concerns a standardized glucose drink, not two hours after an ordinary meal. [ADA's 2026 diagnosis guidance](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes) specifies a 75-g OGTT for these nonpregnant criteria.

Fasting means at least **eight hours without caloric intake** for the diagnostic fasting test. A1C reflects longer-term glucose exposure, whereas plasma glucose is a measurement at a particular time. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis) describes their preparation and limitations. Check the test name on your report before choosing a column.

**Illustrative interpretation:** A laboratory fasting result of 100 mg/dL belongs at the lower boundary of the prediabetes range, not in a “70–100 normal” row. A reading taken after breakfast cannot simply be moved into that fasting column. These examples explain table boundaries; they do not diagnose an individual.

## Child Blood Sugar Levels Chart

A child's age does not justify dismissing a high result as a normal growth-related swing. Children can develop diabetes, and their testing and treatment need pediatric interpretation. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis) describes type 1 diabetes presenting with symptoms such as thirst and frequent urination, and risk-based testing for type 2 diabetes in children and teens.

| Situation | What to establish before interpreting a child's number |
|---|---|
| A laboratory fasting result | Was the test prepared for correctly, and what follow-up does the pediatrician recommend? |
| A home meter reading | Was it part of an existing monitoring plan? A meter cannot establish a new diagnosis. |
| A child already treated for diabetes | What are the child's individualized targets and low-glucose instructions? |
| Symptoms or acute illness | Seek appropriate medical assessment; do not wait for a repeat home result to settle serious symptoms. |

[Testing and home-meter limitations: NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis).

For example, a fasting value of 105 mg/dL is not reclassified as normal merely because another day's result is lower. Share both results and the testing conditions with the clinician. Avoid applying a general adult treatment target to a child or changing insulin from an internet chart. [NIDDK's hypoglycemia guidance](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/preventing-problems/low-blood-glucose-hypoglycemia) emphasizes an agreed plan with the diabetes-care team.

## How Age Affects Blood Sugar

**Screening:** Age is one factor in type 2 diabetes risk. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis) lists **35 or older** among risk considerations, together with other factors. Symptoms warrant testing regardless of a person's age. Discuss when to test with your clinician rather than treating age 45 as a universal starting point or assuming everyone below it is protected.

**Older adults with diabetes:** [ADA's 2026 older-adult guidance, recommendations 13.7a–c](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S277/163921/13-Older-Adults-Standards-of-Care-in-Diabetes-2026) distinguishes health and functional circumstances when setting treatment goals, with attention to hypoglycemia and treatment burden. This is diabetes management advice. It does not make an A1C in the diabetes range a normal diagnostic result because someone has reached a birthday.

**Pregnancy:** Testing uses pregnancy-specific protocols. A screening glucose challenge is different from a diagnostic OGTT, and the timing of each sample matters. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis) describes these tests separately and warns that pregnancy can affect A1C reliability. Ask which protocol your service uses and what each timed value means. A cutoff from one protocol must not be presented as a universal normal post-meal value.

Our [A1C converter](/conditions/a1c-blood-sugar-converter) explains an estimated-average-glucose conversion and its limits. A converted average does not replace a fasting test, describe every daily reading or establish the type of diabetes. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis) notes that glucose diagnostic tests alone do not distinguish diabetes types.

## When Blood Sugar Numbers Are Concerning

**A result in a diagnostic range:** Arrange interpretation and follow-up with the clinician. [ADA](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes) requires confirmation unless hyperglycemia is unequivocal, such as a hyperglycemic crisis. A random laboratory glucose of at least **200 mg/dL with classic hyperglycemia symptoms** can support diagnosis; it is not the same as an isolated home reading after food.

**Low glucose:** [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/preventing-problems/low-blood-glucose-hypoglycemia) uses **below 70 mg/dL** as low for many people with diabetes, while advising an individual target. For someone who can safely swallow and self-treat, its general instructions are **15–20 g glucose or carbohydrate**, then a recheck after **15 minutes**, repeating if still low. Use your agreed plan; this is not a universal dose for every child, medicine or clinical situation. Some medicines require glucose rather than other carbohydrates.

**Severe low glucose:** If a person cannot safely swallow or is unconscious, do not give food or drink by mouth. [NHS guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/low-blood-sugar-hypoglycaemia/) makes that distinction. [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/preventing-problems/low-blood-glucose-hypoglycemia) describes prescribed glucagon for severe episodes and directs calling **911** after glucagon or if a kit is unavailable. Use the local emergency number outside the United States.

**Possible ketoacidosis:** [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/about/diabetic-ketoacidosis.html) advises emergency care for glucose that stays **300 mg/dL or above**, fruity breath, vomiting with inability to keep food or drink down, breathing difficulty, or multiple DKA symptoms. Any of these can warrant action; do not wait for a higher number or require both a threshold and symptoms. DKA can be the first presentation of diabetes.

> **Warning:** A chart cannot decide whether an acutely unwell person is safe. Follow your diabetes emergency plan and seek urgent help for serious symptoms. For routine review, bring the report, test timing, symptoms and current medicines rather than selecting a reassuring row based only on age.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a normal blood sugar level for my age?**

First identify the test and whether the question concerns diagnosis or treatment. The [NIDDK nonpregnant table](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis) uses the ranges above; it does not create a separate higher normal fasting range for seniors. Pregnancy protocols and individual diabetes targets need separate interpretation.

**What is a dangerous blood sugar level?**

Severity depends on symptoms and the situation. Severe hypoglycemia needing another person's help requires prompt treatment; [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/preventing-problems/low-blood-glucose-hypoglycemia) explains emergency glucagon and help. [CDC's DKA emergency signs](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/about/diabetic-ketoacidosis.html) include persistent glucose at least 300 mg/dL and the symptoms listed above. Do not wait for 600 mg/dL or assume a number below a listed threshold excludes an emergency.

**Does blood sugar increase with age?**

Older age is a risk consideration in [NIDDK's screening guidance](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis), not permission to redefine a high laboratory result as healthy. For someone already diagnosed, [ADA](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S277/163921/13-Older-Adults-Standards-of-Care-in-Diabetes-2026) recommends treatment goals tailored to health and functional circumstances.
