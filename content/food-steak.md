# Steak During Pregnancy: Whole Cuts, Ground Beef and Temperature

The safety instruction depends on the type of meat. A steak and a burger should not be given one interchangeable cooking rule. This page uses US consumer guidance and identifies its source.

## The US minimum-temperature distinction

[USDA's safe minimum temperature chart](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart) specifies:

| Food | Minimum internal temperature and rest |
|---|---|
| Beef steak, chop or roast | 145°F (63°C), then at least three minutes' rest |
| Ground beef | 160°F (71°C) |
| Poultry | 165°F (74°C) |

[FDA's cooking guidance for pregnancy](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/cooking-food-safety-moms-be) distinguishes whole cuts from ground meat too. Follow product-specific instructions, especially for products prepared or processed differently from an ordinary whole cut.

## How to check the food you are serving

Use a food thermometer. Check the correct minimum before removing the meat from the heat, and observe the stated rest time. Do not use a restaurant's doneness word, a photograph or the colour alone as a substitute for checking the instruction.

For whole cuts, reach the stated minimum before removing from the heat and then allow the rest period. Do not substitute a lower measured temperature in anticipation of later heating.

## Ask a clear question when eating out

Ask what kind of cut or product is being served and whether the kitchen follows the appropriate temperature and rest instruction. If the answer is unclear, choose an option whose preparation you can verify. Do not substitute the burger temperature for the steak temperature or vice versa.

## Scope and limits

Cooking is one part of food handling; storage and cross-contamination matter as well. A correctly prepared food is not a guarantee against every illness or a claim that a specific diet is right for your pregnancy.
