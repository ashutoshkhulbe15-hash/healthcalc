# What A1C Level Is in the Diabetes Range?

For nonpregnant people, the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK) lists A1C below 5.7% as normal, 5.7–6.4% as prediabetes, and 6.5% or above as in the diabetes range. A result in that range is not, by itself, a diagnosis for most people: in the absence of clear symptoms, the American Diabetes Association (ADA) says diagnosis requires confirmatory testing. A1C is not used to diagnose gestational diabetes or type 1 diabetes.

| A1C result | NIDDK classification for nonpregnant people |
|---|---|
| Below 5.7% | Normal range |
| 5.7–6.4% | Prediabetes range |
| 6.5% or above | Diabetes range; usually requires confirmation |

The A1C test reflects blood glucose over time, but it is not appropriate for every person or circumstance. Conditions affecting red blood cells or hemoglobin can affect results. ADA guidance recommends using plasma glucose criteria when the relationship between A1C and glycemia may be altered, including some hemoglobin variants, pregnancy, HIV and conditions affecting red-cell turnover. If A1C and glucose results do not agree, a clinician should assess possible interference and decide what testing is appropriate.

These are diagnostic criteria, not personalized treatment targets. Do not use this table to diagnose yourself, change medication or infer a personal “normal” target. Discuss test results with a healthcare professional.
