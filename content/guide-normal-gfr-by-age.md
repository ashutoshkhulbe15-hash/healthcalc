# GFR by Age: Understanding eGFR

Kidney filtration estimates tend to decline with age, but age-group averages are not individual “normal” ranges. NIDDK reports the following population mean eGFR values from the U.S. NHANES III survey. These historical group means are context only; they are not diagnostic cutoffs or targets for an individual.

| Age (years) | Population mean eGFR (mL/min/1.73 m²) |
|---|---:|
| 20–29 | 116 |
| 30–39 | 107 |
| 40–49 | 99 |
| 50–59 | 93 |
| 60–69 | 85 |
| 70+ | 75 |

## The equation used by this calculator

The adult creatinine-only CKD-EPI 2021 equation estimates eGFR for people age 18 and older. It uses standardized serum creatinine, age and sex:

`eGFR = 142 × min(SCr/κ, 1)^α × max(SCr/κ, 1)^−1.200 × 0.9938^Age × 1.012 (if female)`

SCr is in mg/dL; κ is 0.7 for females or 0.9 for males; α is −0.241 for females or −0.302 for males. The output is an estimate in mL/min/1.73 m², not a direct measurement. NIDDK says estimates using both creatinine and cystatin C are preferred and more accurate than creatinine alone; urine albumin is also used in a fuller kidney assessment. A single result does not establish kidney disease.
