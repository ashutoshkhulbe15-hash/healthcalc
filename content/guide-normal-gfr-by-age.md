# Normal GFR by Age — Understanding eGFR

## Age is context, not a diagnosis
Filtration tends to change with age, but a single estimated GFR is not a complete kidney assessment. We have removed the unsupported age-by-age averages and uniform fifth-percentile table. Compare results over time with your clinician, who can consider urine albumin, medical history and measurement limitations.

## The equation used by our calculator
The adult creatinine-only CKD-EPI 2021 equation is race-free:

`eGFR = 142 × min(Scr/κ, 1)^α × max(Scr/κ, 1)^−1.200 × 0.9938^Age × 1.012 (if female)`

Scr is standardized creatinine in mg/dL. κ is 0.7 for females or 0.9 for males; α is −0.241 for females or −0.302 for males. No race multiplier is used. The earlier formula printed in this article was from a different equation and has been corrected.

## Important limits
The equation is an adult estimate, not measured filtration. Creatinine can be affected by factors other than kidney function. Where a treatment decision depends on precision, clinicians may use additional assessment, including cystatin C when appropriate. Do not infer an individual “normal for age” target from an invented table.
