# U.S. vitamin D recommended intake reference

This page shows the U.S. age-based recommended daily allowance (RDA) for vitamin D from all sources. NIH Office of Dietary Supplements lists 600 IU (15 mcg) daily for ages 1–70 and 800 IU (20 mcg) for ages 71 and older. For children ages 1–18 and pregnant or breastfeeding teens and women, NIH lists 600 IU. The calculator's age field does not include infants, for whom NIH lists a separate adequate intake. These U.S. population reference values are not individualized supplement doses or deficiency-treatment plans. [NIH Office of Dietary Supplements: Vitamin D, recommended amounts](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-Consumer/)

One microgram equals 40 IU. The calculator's displayed microgram value is a unit conversion of the selected IU reference, not a separate recommendation. [NIH ODS: Vitamin D](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-Consumer/)

## Limits and regional context

An RDA describes an intake reference for healthy people; it does not determine whether an individual is deficient, what dose should be prescribed, or a universal target blood concentration. NIH notes that recommendations differ among countries and professional organizations because their purposes and evidence differ. Follow local guidance and seek individualized advice for a diagnosed deficiency, medical condition or medication question. [NIH ODS professional fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)
