# Vitamin D Calculator: Are You Getting Enough?

This vitamin D calculator is an **age-based US intake reference**, not a deficiency test. Enter your age in whole years, from 1 to 120. The result shows a reference amount for total daily intake; it does not accept diet, supplement or sunlight inputs, and it cannot determine your blood vitamin D level.

The [NIH consumer fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-Consumer/) lists 600 IU daily for ages 1–70 and 800 IU from age 71. Use those numbers to understand the reference shown here, rather than automatically adding that amount as a supplement. The result does not replace an existing treatment plan.

## How Much Vitamin D Do You Need?

> **Key Takeaway:** A recommended dietary allowance (RDA) is a population reference for healthy people, not an individualized treatment dose. Infants have an adequate intake (AI), and are outside this calculator's age range. The upper limit is a separate ceiling, not a target. [NIH definitions and Table 2](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)

| **Age group** | **US daily reference** | **US daily upper limit** |
|---|---|---|
| Birth–6 months | 400 IU (AI) | 1,000 IU |
| 7–12 months | 400 IU (AI) | 1,500 IU |
| 1–3 years | 600 IU (RDA) | 2,500 IU |
| 4–8 years | 600 IU (RDA) | 3,000 IU |
| 9–18 years | 600 IU (RDA) | 4,000 IU |
| 19–70 years | 600 IU (RDA) | 4,000 IU |
| 71+ years | 800 IU (RDA) | 4,000 IU |
| Pregnant/breastfeeding teens and adults | 600 IU (RDA) | 4,000 IU |

Sources: NIH consumer tables, [recommended amounts](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-Consumer/) and [upper limits](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-Consumer/). Count food, beverages and supplements together when comparing intake with the upper limit. A clinician may prescribe a different amount for a defined indication; this table is not a treatment protocol.

The **2024 Endocrine Society prevention guideline** advises against doses above reference intakes for healthy adults under 75 without established treatment or testing indications. It suggests supplementation for selected groups, including adults 75+, pregnancy, children and adolescents, and high-risk prediabetes. These recommendations do not establish one higher dose for everyone. It also advises against routine vitamin D testing in the populations considered. [Endocrine Society guideline summary](https://www.endocrine.org/news-and-advocacy/news-room/2024/endocrine-society-recommends-healthy-adults-take-the-recommended-daily-allowance-of-vitamin-d)

**Regional difference:** NHS advice for the UK is 10 micrograms (400 IU) daily from age 1, with seasonal or year-round supplementation advice depending on age and circumstances. Do not combine that advice with US reference values into a single universal prescription. [NHS vitamin D guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/)

## Vitamin D Sources

**Sun exposure:** Sunlight contributes to vitamin D production, but this tool does not convert minutes outdoors into IU. NIH describes substantial variation and uncertainty about the exposure needed. There is no sunlight-dose estimate in the calculation. [NIH sun exposure section](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)

**Food sources:** The following examples reproduce the serving definitions in NIH's food table; they are not promises about every brand or preparation. [NIH Table 3](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)

| **Food and serving** | **Vitamin D** |
|---|---|
| Sockeye salmon, cooked, 3 ounces | 570 IU |
| Atlantic sardines, canned in oil, drained, 2 sardines | 46 IU |
| Fortified 2% milk, 1 cup | 120 IU |
| Fortified soy, almond or oat milk, 1 cup | 100–144 IU |
| Large scrambled egg | 44 IU |
| Cereal fortified with 10% vitamin D Daily Value, 1 serving | 80 IU |
| White mushrooms, raw, sliced, UV-exposed, ½ cup | 366 IU |

Check your product's Nutrition Facts label and serving size before adding up amounts. Fortification is not universal: the NHS notes that UK cow's milk is generally not fortified with vitamin D. [NHS food sources](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/)

> **Note:** A 25-hydroxyvitamin D blood result needs context. NIH's Food and Nutrition Board categories describe below 12 ng/mL as deficient, 12 to below 20 as generally inadequate, and 20 or more as generally adequate for most healthy people; above 50 may be associated with adverse effects. These are not a universal personal treatment target. [NIH Table 1](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)

> **Warning:** Excess supplement intake can cause high blood calcium and serious harm. The NHS describes potential damage to the kidneys and heart. Follow an individual prescription if you have one; do not use the upper-limit column as permission to take that amount. [NHS excessive intake advice](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/)

> **Practical use:** Keep the calculator result, your food labels and your supplement labels separate. It reports a reference intake, not how much additional vitamin D you need. If your question concerns treatment or a blood result, take that information to your clinician or pharmacist rather than relying on an age-only output.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I know if I'm vitamin D deficient?**

This calculator cannot answer that. It displays no blood-test interpretation. The 2024 prevention guideline does not recommend routine testing in otherwise healthy people and does not identify universal blood targets for disease prevention. An established clinical indication is a different question. [Endocrine Society summary](https://www.endocrine.org/news-and-advocacy/news-room/2024/endocrine-society-recommends-healthy-adults-take-the-recommended-daily-allowance-of-vitamin-d)

**How much vitamin D should I take daily?**

Read the US intake table as a reference for the total, not as an instruction to add a supplement. The result changes with age only. It does not incorporate a prescription, a medical condition, your diet or your current supplement. Discuss those details when asking about a personal dose. [NIH consumer guidance](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-Consumer/)

**Can I get enough vitamin D from food?**

Review the food-source table and the actual labels on foods you use. In the UK, NHS guidance says food alone can make meeting vitamin D needs difficult and advises considering a 400 IU supplement during autumn and winter. That is regional guidance, not a claim that everyone everywhere needs the same supplement. [NHS seasonal advice](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/)
