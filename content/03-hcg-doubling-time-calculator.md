# hCG Doubling Time Calculator: Change Between Two Blood Tests

This calculator reports the percentage change between two quantitative hCG blood results and a mathematical doubling or halving time. It does not label a pregnancy healthy, diagnose pregnancy loss, identify twins or determine whether a pregnancy is ectopic.

## Entering your results

Enter the earlier blood result first and the later result second, both in mIU/mL. Enter the actual number of hours between the blood draws, including part-hours if known. For example, 2 days and 3 hours is 51 hours; two calendar dates alone do not establish an exact interval.

Check units and collection times on your laboratory reports. Do not combine urine-test line darkness with quantitative blood values: these are different measurements.

## How the arithmetic works

**Percentage change = (second value ÷ first value − 1) × 100.**

For an increase, **doubling time = elapsed hours × ln(2) ÷ ln(second value ÷ first value).**

For a decrease, the analogous calculation uses ln(first value ÷ second value) and describes a halving time. Unchanged results have no finite doubling time. The tool displays “Not applicable” rather than infinity or a misleading negative doubling time.

| First result | Second result | Elapsed hours | Arithmetic result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 200 | 48 | 100% increase; 48-hour doubling |
| 100 | 150 | 48 | 50% increase; about 82.1-hour doubling |
| 100 | 100 | 48 | No change; doubling time not applicable |
| 200 | 100 | 48 | 50% decrease; 48-hour halving |

These examples demonstrate the calculation, not clinical categories. Two points cannot show that the same rate will continue, so the result is not a forecast of your next test.

## Clinical interpretation needs more than arithmetic

[NICE NG126](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG126/chapter/diagnosis-of-viable-intrauterine-pregnancy-and-of-tubal-ectopic-pregnancy) uses serial hCG with symptoms and ultrasound when pregnancy location is unknown. hCG alone must not determine location, and symptoms deserve attention regardless of an earlier result. Follow your care team's testing plan.

## Symptoms take priority

Seek urgent assessment for pain or bleeding if pregnancy is possible. Sudden severe abdominal pain with marked dizziness or fainting requires emergency care. Do not wait for a repeat test or a calculator result. [NHS ectopic-pregnancy symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ectopic-pregnancy/symptoms/).
