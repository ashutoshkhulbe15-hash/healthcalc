# Measuring Body Fat at Home: What an Estimate Can Tell You

Home methods estimate body composition; they do not directly diagnose health or measure every fat depot. Results from different methods should not be treated as interchangeable or as precise personal measurements.

## BMI and body composition

BMI is a screening measure based on height and weight. CDC notes that BMI does not directly measure body fat or distinguish fat from lean mass, and should be considered alongside other clinical factors. A body-fat percentage estimate also has measurement and model limitations; it does not replace a clinical assessment.

## Circumference equations

Our body-fat calculator applies a circumference-based equation to entered measurements. Such an output is an estimate produced by a formula, not a direct measurement or a diagnosis. Follow the calculator's specified measurements and units; changing the measurement site changes the input and can change the estimate. The equation's source population may not represent every person.

## Scope of this guide

This page does not compare skinfold, bioelectrical-impedance or imaging methods, and does not assign a universal accuracy margin or rank a “best” method. The claims previously made about accuracy and home tracking were removed because this review did not verify a directly applicable source for them.

If a body-composition number is relevant to a medical decision, discuss the measurement method and result with a qualified health professional. For a general screening measure, see the [BMI calculator](/body-metrics/bmi-calculator). For the equation estimate, see the [body-fat calculator](/fitness/body-fat-calculator).
