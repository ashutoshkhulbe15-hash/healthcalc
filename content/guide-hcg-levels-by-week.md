# hCG Levels by Week: Reference Examples and Their Limits

A quantitative blood test measures hCG concentration. A week-by-week table provides context, but it cannot classify an individual pregnancy as healthy, identify its location or confirm twins.

## A source-specific reference table

The examples below come from [MedlinePlus's quantitative hCG blood-test reference](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003510.htm). They are not universal cutoffs. Laboratory reference ranges and pregnancy dating can differ.

| Pregnancy week | Example hCG range, mIU/mL |
|---|---|
| 3 | 5–72 |
| 4 | 10–708 |
| 5 | 217–8,245 |
| 6 | 152–32,177 |
| 7 | 4,059–153,767 |
| 8 | 31,366–149,094 |
| 9 | 59,109–135,901 |
| 10 | 44,186–170,409 |

MedlinePlus describes hCG rising during the first trimester and subsequently falling. A concentration outside a displayed interval needs interpretation with the lab report and clinical circumstances. A value inside it does not provide a guarantee.

## Why the starting value and timing matter

A serial result should include both concentrations and the collection times. For example, 200 to 300 is a 50% rise. Across 48 hours, the mathematical doubling time is about 82.1 hours; across 72 hours, it is about 123.1 hours. The same pair of values gives a different estimate when the interval changes.

Use the [hCG change calculator](/pregnancy/hcg-doubling-time-calculator) for this arithmetic. It does not assign a normal/abnormal result from doubling time alone.

## What serial tests cannot settle

[NICE guidance for pregnancy of unknown location](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG126/chapter/diagnosis-of-viable-intrauterine-pregnancy-and-of-tubal-ectopic-pregnancy) combines serial hCG with clinical assessment and ultrasound. Even a substantial rise does not exclude ectopic pregnancy. Do not interpret a chart as permission to ignore new symptoms or to change a follow-up plan.

This page does not supply ranges by days past ovulation or a separate twin chart. The linked reference intervals cannot confirm the number of babies or fetal sex.

## Getting help

Contact a healthcare service for bleeding or pain when pregnancy is possible. Severe sudden pain with dizziness or fainting needs emergency care, regardless of the hCG value. [NHS symptom guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ectopic-pregnancy/symptoms/).

*This guide supplies reference context and worked arithmetic examples, not a diagnosis or individualized test schedule.*
