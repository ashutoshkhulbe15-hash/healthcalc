# Tuna During Pregnancy: Follow the FDA Category

“Tuna” includes types placed in different categories in the current [FDA/EPA advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/questions-answers-fdaepa-advice-about-eating-fish-those-who-might-become-or-are-pregnant-or):

| Type in FDA/EPA advice | Category | US weekly advice for adults who are or may become pregnant |
|---|---|---|
| Canned light tuna (includes skipjack) | Best Choices | 2–3 servings total per week from Best Choices fish |
| Albacore (white) tuna | Good Choices | 1 serving that week, and no other fish |
| Yellowfin tuna | Good Choices | 1 serving that week, and no other fish |
| Bigeye tuna | Choices to Avoid | Do not eat |

One serving for an adult is four ounces. The categories apply to the type as identified in the guidance; if the product or species is unclear, check its label or ask the seller. The weekly amounts apply to fish overall as stated in each category, not as an additional allowance for each kind of tuna.

These are US mercury-based food-choice recommendations. For fish caught by family or friends, check local advisories. The chart does not guarantee the mercury content of an individual serving.

See the [general fish guide](/guides/fish-seafood-pregnancy) and the FDA/EPA page for all species and current details.

*Educational information based on US FDA/EPA guidance.*
